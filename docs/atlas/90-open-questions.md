| OPEN-29 | What does the clustering group: individual posts, or threads? Measured 2026-10-18 on three real days of r/sailing (1,851 posts and comments in 109 threads), with no model calls. (1) Grouping individual posts and comments by meaning, as specified, fails: most comments ("Awesome, thanks!", "Maybe, maybe not") carry no subject of their own, only their thread does. 86% of posts joined no group, so the three-day rule would delete them; what did group was mostly generic replies. With one group allowed, the whole first day became a single grab-bag that the agent rightly called noise, and 372 real posts were deleted (local test data only). (2) Embedding each comment together with its thread's title made groups that are each just one thread, and still left 42% of posts ungrouped and due for deletion, mostly comments in smaller threads, which would badly undercount engagement. (3) Grouping threads, by the text of the post that starts each one, found real subjects: five boat-theft threads together, four learning-to-sail threads, first-boat buying, Cal 29 seaworthiness, the Turkish coast. 65 groups from 109 threads; 43 threads stood alone. The recommendation: make the thread the unit. Each day, new threads are grouped by meaning; every group, including a thread on its own, goes to the tagging agent, which attaches it to an existing topic, mints one, or calls it noise; every comment takes its thread's topic, including comments arriving days later; noise deletes the thread with its comments. This keeps every real conversation and its engagement, and topics still span many threads because the agent attaches new threads to existing topics. It changes the specified three-day rule: instead of 'a post that joins no cluster in three days is deleted', each thread gets a decision on the day it starts. Cost: about one agent call per group, roughly 20 a day, so around 1,100 calls and an estimated $10 for a first start (not yet measured). The alternative that keeps the rule as specified is (2), accepting that around four in ten posts are deleted. | [21](20-data-model/21-topics.md) |
