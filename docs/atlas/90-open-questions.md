| OPEN-13 | For now, nothing is pre-seeded: the first `docker compose up` prewarms from live data (fetch eight weeks from Arctic Shift, embed, place, tag day by day) and the reviewer waits it out (Brian, 2026-10-18). Still open, to revisit once prewarm is built and timed: (1) how long it takes. **Measured 2026-10-18, build step 2 (fetch and embedding, before tagging exists):** about 35–50 seconds a day on a laptop, so roughly 40–45 minutes for the eight weeks, plus a one-time 130 MB model download; tagging in step 3 will add model calls on top; (2) the demo then depends on Arctic Shift being up and on the OpenRouter key working at the moment the reviewer starts it, and the tagging calls during prewarm are paid from that key; (3) If prewarm proves slow or fragile, the fallback is committing the computed database. | [04](04-environment.md) |
| OPEN-29 | What does the clustering group, and when is a thread encoded? Measured 2026-10-18 on three real days of r/sailing (1,851 posts and comments in 109 threads), no model calls. Grouping individual posts fails: most comments ("Awesome, thanks!") carry no subject of their own, 86% joined no group and would be deleted, and with one group allowed the whole first day became a grab-bag the agent called noise. Brian then said a comment belongs with its post, so the thread should be encoded as one unit. Measured three ways of doing that: the post's text alone gave 65 groups (43 threads alone); the post's meaning averaged with its comments' made one 51-thread blob, because generic replies pull everything to the middle; the post and its top five comments written out as one text gave the most coherent groups (sail handling and rigging, boats to buy or charter, the thefts, sailing trips), 57 groups with 39 threads alone. Brian also said comments arriving later are a heat signal, and so is an old thread that suddenly gets comments again; he is not fully against re-encoding a thread. The question left is whether a thread is encoded once or again as comments arrive. Recommendation: encode each thread once, three days after it starts, as the post plus its top comments; group that day's three-day-old threads; the agent attaches, mints or calls noise. Comments after day three take the thread's topic and count as engagement on the day they are written, which is the heat Brian describes, and needs no re-encoding. A revived thread is counted separately each day per topic (proposed: a thread more than seven days old that gets new comments), so the prediction can see a topic waking up. Re-encoding is not recommended, because it could move a thread to a different topic after the fact and rewrite that topic's past engagement, and it costs an embedding plus an agent decision each time. The one case it would catch, a revived thread whose conversation has moved to a new subject, is rare and can be found later from the comments' own embeddings without re-encoding the thread. This replaces the specified rule 'a post that joins no cluster in three days is deleted' with 'each thread gets one decision three days after it starts'. | [21](20-data-model/21-topics.md) |
